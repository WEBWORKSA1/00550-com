Lucky numbers for business in China

Updated {{ site.time | date: "%Y-%m-%d" }} · 8 min read

Rule of thumb: favour 8, 6 and 9, avoid 4 (and 14, 74, 94), never price at 250, and don't launch in the 7th lunar month.

1. Pricing

Western retail ends prices in 9 or 99. Chinese-facing retail often ends in 8 (¥88, ¥168, ¥888) or 6. 168 (一路发, prosper all the way) and 518 (我要发, I'll prosper) are especially popular. Avoid 4-heavy prices like ¥44 or ¥444, and 250 (二百五 means “idiot”).

2. Phone numbers & hotlines

The ending matters most. Numbers ending 8, 88, 168 or 6688 read as prosperous; endings like 14 (要死) or 74 (气死) read badly. In many markets carriers and resellers sell “beautiful numbers” (靓号) at a premium.

3. Addresses & floors

Many buildings skip floors 4, 14, 24 (and sometimes 13). Offices on floor 8 or 18, or at number 8 or 168, carry positive associations.

4. Launch & signing dates

  • Look for dates containing 6, 8 or 9, such as the 8th, 18th and 28th.
  • Avoid the 7th lunar month (Ghost Month). Use our Lucky Date Finder.
  • Shopping festivals: 6·18, 11·11 (Singles' Day), 12·12.

5. Gifts & red envelopes

Give in pairs, avoid sets of four, and pick amounts like 66, 88, 168, 520 (romantic) or 888. Avoid clocks, which sound like 送终 (attending a deathbed).

6. Brand and product names

Numbers in product names (e.g. model “4”) and the Chinese transliteration of your brand can carry unintended meanings. Screen both Mandarin and Cantonese readings before launch.
