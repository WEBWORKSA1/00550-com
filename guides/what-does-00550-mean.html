What does 00550 mean in Chinese?

Updated {{ site.time | date: "%Y-%m-%d" }} · 6 min read

Quick answer: Read formally, 00550 is líng líng wǔ wǔ líng (零零五五零). In Chinese number slang, where 0 = 你 (you) and 5 = 我 (me), it reads 你你我我你, “you, you, me, me, you”. That makes it a playful number about two people.

1. The formal reading

Each digit is read individually, as with phone numbers: 零 (líng) 零 (líng) 五 (wǔ) 五 (wǔ) 零 (líng). In Cantonese: ling4 ling4 ng5 ng5 ling4.

2. The digits' symbolism

DigitSymbolismSound-alike
0Wholeness, the circle, a fresh start良 liáng “good”; 你 nǐ “you” (slang)
5Five Elements, Five Blessings, imperial centre我/吾 “me”; 无 “without”; Cantonese 唔 “not”

3. The slang reading: “you & me”

Chinese texting culture swaps digits for similar-sounding words. The best-known code, 520 (wǔ èr líng), sounds like 我爱你 (wǒ ài nǐ, “I love you”), so 5 → 我 (me) and 0 → 你 (you). The same logic appears in 04551 (你是我唯一, “you are my only one”). Applied to 00550, you get 你你我我你, a back-and-forth between “you” and “me”.

4. Things to be aware of

  • 55 = 呜呜 (crying) in internet slang, so some readers may see a teary “you, you, boo-hoo, you”. In formal contexts (codes, IDs) nobody reads it that way.
  • Cantonese: 5 sounds like 唔 (not), which gives different puns in Hong Kong, Macau and Guangdong.
  • Leading zeros: in numeric domains and phone numbers, leading 0s are considered less prestigious than 8s, but they make a string easy to type and remember.

5. Where you might see 00550

Five-digit numbers appear as stock codes, postal and product codes, extension numbers and domain names. Any such use is unrelated to this website. 00550.com is an independent culture and tools site. See our affiliation disclosure.
