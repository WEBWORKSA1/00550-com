Why numbers matter so much in China

Mandarin has only about 400 base syllables, so most numbers sound like other words. 8 (bā) echoes 发 (fā, prosper), so it is priced like gold. 4 (sì) echoes 死 (sǐ, death), and buildings often skip the fourth floor. The Beijing Olympics opened on 08-08-08 at 8:08 pm.

This sound-alike thinking shapes phone-number and license-plate markets, retail prices, wedding dates, stock-code nicknames and even domain names. It is also a playful everyday language: young people text 520 for “I love you” and 88 for “bye-bye”.

Frequently asked questions

What does 00550 mean in Chinese?

Formally it's líng líng wǔ wǔ líng (零零五五零). In texting slang, 0 stands for 你 (you) and 5 for 我 (me), so 00550 playfully reads 你你我我你, “you, you, me, me, you”. It's a number about two people. Note that 55 alone can mean crying (呜呜) online, and in Cantonese 5 can sound like “not”. Full guide.

What are the luckiest and unluckiest Chinese numbers?

8 (prosper), 6 (smooth) and 9 (long-lasting) are the luckiest. 4 (death) is the most avoided, followed by combinations like 14 and 74. 250 is an insult. See digits 0–9.

How is the luck score calculated?

We weight each digit by its traditional symbolism, add bonuses and penalties for meaningful pairs (like 28 “easy prosperity” or 14 “want to die”), detect well-known slang phrases, and give extra weight to the final digit. It's a cultural guide, not a prediction.

Do Cantonese and Mandarin readings differ?

Yes, a lot. 5 sounds like “me” in Mandarin but “not” in Cantonese, so 54 is “I die” in one and “won't die” in the other. Our decoder shows both pronunciations.
